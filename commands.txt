1) Boundary ambiguity + 2s context is manufacturing false positives (very likely).
Even in clean songs, there are micro-gaps and transitions. Your own labels show lots of tight boundaries and occasional 1-frame overlaps 

Mine_labels

. A 2s window centered on “gap” frames still contains strong evidence of the previous/next singer, so the model predicts them. This is not “wrong” for a vocal-presence task, but it looks wrong if your ground truth treats those frames as “nobody.”
Elimination test: take your “Mine” label segments and create a boundary mask: mark frames within ±3 frames (±120ms) of any segment boundary as “don’t score.” Evaluate spam rate only on the interior frames. If the spam collapses inside segments but stays high near boundaries, it’s mostly a label-definition mismatch + context effect.

2) Your decoder is turning small spikes into long spam (very likely).
You’re doing smoothing, hysteresis, minimum-on duration, and gap filling. Those steps can absolutely make “a few frames where Yujin is a close runner-up” become “Yujin is active across the pause.” The fact that you see spam between members is classic “gap fill + hysteresis” behavior.
Elimination test: run inference on Mine with “raw-ish” decode: k_smooth=1, min_on_frames=1, max_gap_frames=0, topk=1. If spam drops a lot, it’s decoder amplification, not the model hallucinating.

3) Training prior/imbalance makes Yujin the default attractor (plausible and common).
If Yujin has more clean, high-SNR examples, or your weighting makes her positives “easier,” then in ambiguous frames the model defaults to her. That explains “Yujin looks perfect on her lines and shows up elsewhere,” plus “Wonyoung/Liz are allergic.”
Elimination test: compute per-member average probability across a song restricted to interior frames of other members. If Yujin’s baseline is systematically higher than everyone else’s, that’s a calibration/prior issue. Then fix with per-class thresholds (you don’t need retraining to prove this).

4) Mic-distance / filtered parts harming Wonyoung/Liz identity (plausible).
If Wonyoung/Liz have more “off-mic” or filtered segments in your training set (or your isolated stems), their embeddings may drift, causing low confidence and more confusion. But this alone doesn’t create spam everywhere; it mainly creates “model refuses to commit,” and your decoder/fusion then fills the vacuum with the dominant class.
Elimination test: compare RMS and spectral centroid (or just your printed RMS) between members’ labeled segments on Mine and a couple other songs. If Wonyoung/Liz segments are consistently quieter or more “air-filtered,” you’ll see it.
